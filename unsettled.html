The counterpart to the settled floor

What the standard picture does not know

The argument this page makes is not that the frontier is right. It is narrower and much harder to answer: the received account has holes in it that its own practitioners publish, and a position with holes that size cannot honestly be delivered as certainty. That claim does not need the micronova to be true. It survives whether or not it is.

One

The ledger, in the field's own words.

None of these come from outside. Every one is a named, published, actively worked problem, and several have their own conference sessions. This is what the confident version is built on top of.

Named open problems in the mainstream account
The gapWhat is actually admitted
Most of the universe is unidentifiedDark matter and dark energy together are put at around 95 per cent of the mass and energy budget. Neither has been detected directly. The name is a placeholder for a discrepancy, not a description of a substance.
Gravity has no quantum descriptionGeneral relativity and quantum mechanics have not been reconciled and there is no accepted theory of quantum gravity. The two best-tested frameworks in physics contradict each other where they overlap.
The cosmological constantThe value predicted from quantum field theory and the value observed differ by a factor usually quoted around ten to the power of 120. It is routinely described as the worst prediction in physics.
The Hubble tensionThe universe's expansion rate measured near and measured far do not agree, the gap has widened rather than closed as measurements improved, and it is unresolved.
The Sun's own interiorThe solar abundance problem. Standard solar models built with the measured surface composition fail against helioseismology on convection zone depth, surface helium and sound speed. Described in the literature as long-standing and exasperating. Set out here.
Why the ice ages keep time the way they doThe orbital variations are calculated and they line up with the record, which is why the theory holds. What is not explained is which one dominates. The strongest rhythm in the ice record sits near 100,000 years, and that is precisely where the orbital forcing is weakest of the three. The field calls it the 100,000-year problem and it is open. Alongside it sits the Mid-Pleistocene Transition, where the dominant beat switched from 41,000 to 100,000 years with no corresponding change in the forcing at all. So "the ice ages are explained by orbital cycles" is a half-truth: the clock is calculated, the response to it is not.
How chondrules formedThe millimetre melt droplets that make up most of the mass of ordinary meteorites. Their formation mechanism is still unsolved after decades of work.
The mammoth steppeThe productivity paradox: reconciling that mammal community with the vegetation the ground appears able to produce. Open, and the mechanism behind the productivity is actively contested. Set out here.
Which stars dusted the EarthIron-60 arrival is measured. No progenitor is identified, the Scorpius-Centaurus association is a candidate rather than a finding, and published distances span roughly 10 to over 100 parsecs.
What counts as a novaThe taxonomy of stellar outbursts is being rewritten as survey coverage improves. Luminous red novae were refiled as a subclass; fast blue optical transients carry three names for one phenomenon; micronova became a formal class only in 2022. Set out here.
Known

Set those beside the sentence a confident dismissal opens with, and the mismatch is the point. A field that cannot say what most of the universe is made of, cannot make its two best theories agree, and cannot model the interior of the nearest star, is not in a position to close a question by tone.

Two

But uncertainty has a gradient, and the gradient is what makes the argument work.

This is the discipline that separates the position above from an anything-goes one, and it has to be stated or the whole thing turns to mush.

"Not certain" does not mean "all equally uncertain". The age of the Solar System and the nature of dark energy are not in the same epistemic condition and pretending otherwise would be a worse error than the overconfidence being complained about. If everything were equally open, then nothing on this site would be worth arguing either, including the parts that are strong.

Which is why this site is built as a ladder rather than a list

Five layers, ranked, with the strongest first and the weakest last, and the argument deliberately arranged so it does not depend on the weak end. That structure is not decoration. It is the thing that lets a claim be pressed hard on the floor and held loosely at the frontier without either half contaminating the other.

Same discipline applied here: the ledger above is real, and it does not license any particular replacement. A hole in an account is a reason to stop asserting certainty. It is not evidence for whatever you would like to put in the hole.

Worked the other way, this is also why the two examples below matter more than the ledger does. One correction runs against the received view. One runs against the frontier. If only ever one direction came up, the method would be broken.

Three · the correction that runs against the received view

Change the star and you do change the orbits. Here is the arithmetic.

The idea that a planetary system is a fixed clockwork independent of its star turns out to be wrong in a way that is standard, published, and carries a formula.

The Sun is losing mass continuously, through radiation and through the wind, at roughly 9 parts in ten thousand billion of itself per year. As it does, every orbit in the system widens by the same fractional amount, and every orbital period lengthens at twice that rate. The relationship is direct: lose a given fraction of the star and the orbits expand by that fraction.

QuantityFigureIn the mind's eye
Earth's orbit widening nowAbout 1.5 cm per yearRoughly the width of a fingernail, every year, for the whole orbit.
Accumulated over the Solar System's lifeAbout 62,000 kmNearly five Earth diameters. Widely quoted as "about 7,000 miles", which is a figure that contradicts the 1.5 cm per year it is usually printed beside. Run the arithmetic yourself.
Saturn, same mechanismAbout 14 cm per yearScales with distance, so the outer system feels it most.
Known

And that turns a rhetorical point into a measurable bound

If the Sun sheds a shell, that is a large mass loss in a short time, and the orbits must respond. Which means the question stops being whether the environment can reach the orbits and becomes how much mass can have been shed before the orbital record forbids it.

That is a real constraint and it cuts both ways. It gives the shedding hypothesis a quantitative prediction it did not have, and it puts a ceiling on the size of any event that can be proposed. Nobody on either side of this argument appears to have set that number down. It belongs on the list of things worth checking.

Two related things worth stating plainly, because both get treated as fringe and neither is. Resonance is central, not exotic: Io, Europa and Ganymede are locked in a four to two to one chain, Neptune and Pluto in three to two, and the seven planets of TRAPPIST-1 form a resonant chain throughout. Kepler derived his third law out of an explicitly musical project, and the harmonic reading of orbital ratios is where the law came from rather than a later decoration on it. And magnetism is not an alternative to the standard account of star formation, it is load-bearing inside it: removing angular momentum is the central problem of making a star at all, magnetic braking is how the standard picture solves it, and protostellar jets are magnetically launched. Where the electrical account departs is in making current the primary energy source rather than gravitational collapse, and that step is not supported. The magnetic half is orthodox.

Four · the correction that runs the other way

The galaxies that broke cosmology did not break cosmology.

This one is included precisely because it goes against the argument the rest of the page is making. A ledger that only ever finds in one direction is not a ledger.

In 2023 the telescope found compact red objects in the early universe that looked far too massive to have assembled that fast, and the headline wrote itself. The finding was real and the tension was real. It has since been substantially resolved, and the resolution is specific rather than hand-waved.

What changedThe finding
The masses were overestimatedWork published in early 2026 put the black hole masses too high by around a factor of a hundred, because dense surrounding gas was inflating the measurement.
The vanishing act was an artefactThese objects appear to disappear at closer distances. They do not. Their host galaxies dim below visibility with distance, leaving only the bright core, which is a known observational effect rather than a population changing.
What they areRead now as supermassive black holes in a violent early growth phase, with host galaxies present but invisible. Several competing models for the growth rate remain in play.

Two honest halves

The first: the framing was never right even at the peak of the tension. The difficulty was about how fast galaxies assemble inside the model, not about whether there was a beginning. The redshifts that make these objects early are the same measurements that evidence the expansion. An anomaly inside a framework is not an argument against the framework.

The second, which cuts the other way: the loud announcement travelled enormously further than the quiet resolution. Three years on, the broken-cosmology version is still the one in circulation. That asymmetry is real, it is exactly the pattern that makes people distrust the institution, and it is not the fault of the people who noticed.

Same discipline applies to the age of the Solar System. It is not a guess. It comes from lead-lead dating of calcium and aluminium rich inclusions in meteorites and currently sits at about 4,567 million years with a stated uncertainty of a fraction of a million. What is fair to say is narrower than the number looks: it dates a specific event, the condensation of those inclusions, and it depends on assumptions about closed-system behaviour and on an isotope ratio that was itself revised once measurement improved. A precisely measured quantity with model assumptions inside it is a different thing from a guess, and also a different thing from a fact of nature.

Five

The pattern got cheap. The check did not.

On whether anyone with a connection can now out-pattern-match a specialist, the answer is genuinely yes on one half and genuinely no on the other, and the split is the useful part.

The yes is real and it is structural. Specialists are deep, not wide, and the correspondences that matter most often sit between fields where nobody is looking. This site contains one found exactly that way: iron-60 in seabed sediment puts nearby supernovae at about two to three million years ago and again near seven, and heliospheric modelling from a completely unrelated method puts cloud crossings at about two million and near seven. Both mainstream. Neither field appears to have deliberately compared them. That is not a finding a specialist was ever going to be positioned to make, and the same is true of the boundary-crossers on the record of overturned defaults.

And the no is just as structural

Patterns are cheap and most of them are noise. The reason careful work is slow is not gatekeeping, it is that killing your own good-looking pattern is expensive and nobody enjoys it. What changed is the price of finding a pattern. What did not change is the price of checking one, and a tool that shifts one without the other makes the bottleneck worse rather than better.

This site is evidence for both halves at once, which is why it says so. The machine that helped build it produced the correspondence above, and in the same stretch of work stated a false claim about stellar nucleosynthesis as settled physics, declared a true claim false by checking the wrong object, quoted a 27-month measurement as a constant, and rated an observed phenomenon as unobserved. Every one of those errors had the same shape: confidence not earned by the checking behind it. The corrections are logged in the open for that reason.

Which lands on the one honest instruction that comes out of this page. The received account has real holes and should not be delivered as certainty. That conclusion is only worth anything held by someone applying the same standard to themselves, and the expensive half is still the checking. On this site the largest outstanding gap is exactly there: most of the sources behind it have been read as abstracts rather than as papers, and that is named on the citation page rather than hidden.

Six · the move underneath several entries above

Sufficiency is not exhaustiveness.

A model that accounts for the observations shows that the observations are accounted for. It does not show there is nothing else present. "Well described by X" never establishes "nothing but X", and that gap is where several of the entries on this page live.

The counterexample is us, and it is the only case anyone can check from the inside. Take the outside view of a human being: biochemistry, firing patterns, behaviour. What comes out is a complete predictive model with no term in it for there being something it is like to be that person. The description does not fail. It simply has nothing to say about the thing each of us is most certain of.

The account was sufficient. It was not exhaustive, and the gap sat in the part we know best.

What follows from that is narrow. There is no method for detecting inner experience that is not analogy. Each of us knows we are something because we are here, and extends it outward on the strength of resemblance. The inference weakens with distance: other people, then mammals, then octopuses where it gets genuinely interesting and unresolved, then insects. By the time the subject is something as unlike us as a star, there is nothing left for the analogy to run on. That is a statement about our instruments, not about the star.

And the question has a literature rather than being a joke. Panpsychism is held by working philosophers including Galen Strawson and Philip Goff, in versions David Chalmers takes seriously. Integrated Information Theory assigns a quantity to physical systems and does not obviously exclude non-biological ones. It is also sharply contested: over a hundred researchers signed an open letter in 2023 calling it pseudoscience, and there was substantial pushback to that letter in turn. Live and contested is the honest label, and this ledger puts that label on plenty of things.

Disputed

The same rule catches two claims already on this page

"The ice ages are explained by orbital cycles" and "the Sun is understood" are both sufficiency claims wearing the clothes of exhaustive ones. The orbital arithmetic lines up with the record while the response to it stays open, and the standard solar model reproduces a great deal while disagreeing with helioseismology on depth, helium and sound speed. What a model covers and what is there are two different lists, and the second one is longer more often than the first one admits.
