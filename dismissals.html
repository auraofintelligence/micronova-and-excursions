Fourteen stock replies

The waffle catalogue

Every one of these has been said in place of an argument. Some reach a defensible conclusion by an indefensible route, and a bad argument for a true conclusion is still a bad argument. Where a real objection is hiding inside one, the better version is marked, and those better versions get made properly on the next page.

  1. The Sun cannot go nova, it has no binary companion.
    The problem
    It answers a claim nobody made. A classical nova needs a dead star with a strange surface stealing hydrogen from a neighbour. The proposed event is not that. Correct physics, wrong target, and it is the single most common thing said about this topic.
    The version that works
    The proposed solar mechanism has no worked model, and borrowing the white dwarf's name does not import the white dwarf's physics.
  2. Micronova is a made-up word.
    The problem
    False. Nature, April 2022. It has been standard vocabulary in astronomy for four years and now appears in classification papers.
    The version that works
    The 2022 micronova is a different object, so the word's adoption is not evidence for the solar claim.
  3. Ben Davidson is a lawyer, not a scientist.
    The problem
    An attack on a person, and a tell. Credentials are not evidence and never have been. When this turns up in an answer it is usually doing the work a citation should be doing. It is also selectively applied: nobody discounts a correct claim from an amateur astronomer, and the history of the field is full of them.
    The version that works
    Name the claim that is wrong, and show why.
  4. It's pseudoscience.
    The problem
    A verdict standing in for an argument. The word describes a method rather than a conclusion, so it has to be earned by showing the method failing.
    The version that works
    That case can partly be made here. The forecast window has moved more than once, and a forecast that survives every observation by widening has stopped doing work. Say that instead.
  5. There is no evidence.
    The problem
    False, and lazy in a specific way. Miyake events, superflare statistics, Laschamps, the measured field decay, the growing South Atlantic anomaly. All mainstream, all published, all relevant.
    The version that works
    What is usually meant is "there is no evidence for the mechanism", which is defensible and is a different sentence. Say the sentence you mean.
  6. There's no 12,000-year cycle in the record.
    The problem
    Nearly right, and worth stating properly. "No cycle has been demonstrated" and "the record shows there is no cycle" are different claims, and only the first is supportable. The palaeo record is sparse and dating uncertainty grows with depth.
    The version that works
    Check the ladder rung by rung and say what you find. Two accepted, three proposed and never confirmed, two with nothing behind them.
  7. That's just the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis, which was debunked.
    The problem
    Two errors. They are separate claims and the micronova argument does not depend on an impact. And "debunked" overstates a live dispute.
    The version that works
    The direction of travel is real: the Greenland crater dated to about 58 million years, the platinum spike reattributed to Icelandic fissure volcanism, and two supporting papers retracted in 2025 and 2026. So leaning on it as corroboration now costs more than it pays.
  8. Scientists would have noticed.
    The problem
    Assumes the conclusion, and the record is against it. Miyake events sat in tree rings until 2012. Superflare frequency on stars like ours was underestimated by one to two orders of magnitude until 2024. The pole motion forced the first out-of-cycle navigation model update in 2019.
    The version that works
    Noticing is a process with a rate, and on this exact topic the rate has been slow and the corrections have all gone the same way.
  9. The field has been weakening for ages, it's normal.
    The problem
    True and incomplete. Normal is the right first read: the field has been through many such episodes.
    The version that works
    Normal is not the same as harmless. What is new is the exposure. An ordinary storm against a weaker field, hitting a grid full of long conductors and large transformers, is not a scenario the geological record can reassure anyone about.
  10. A pole reversal takes thousands of years, nothing to worry about.
    The problem
    Confuses a reversal with an excursion, and overstates the consensus on timing. Excursions are faster and come back to the same polarity, and the literature on how fast a field can change has been revised towards faster, not slower.
    The version that works
    The flip is not the point. The low point on the way is.
  11. Correlation is not causation.
    The problem
    True, and usually deployed to end a conversation rather than advance one. The argument here is explicitly a coincidence-counting argument, so this objection has to do actual work.
    The version that works
    The events were dated and selected by people who already knew what the pattern was meant to be, so the pattern may live in the selection. That is a real objection and close to decisive. Make it.
  12. Peer review would have caught it.
    The problem
    A bad argument in general, and here also a false one. Solar effects on climate oscillations, solar effects on earthquake timing, cosmic-ray effects on cloud and volcanic processes: all have peer-reviewed literature, much of it contested, some of it cited accurately in the frontier material.
    The version that works
    The dispute is over weight and interpretation, not over whether anything is published. Argue the weight.
  13. This is doomsday prepper stuff.
    The problem
    Guilt by association, and it smuggles in a link that is not there. The physics claim and the preparation advice come apart cleanly. Grid hardening, transformer spares, local water, local food and offline capability are justified by the risk of a Carrington-class storm alone, which is already a planning assumption for governments and insurers.
    The version that works
    Notice the move being pulled: the preparation gets dismissed by attacking the physics, and the physics gets dismissed by attacking the preparation. Neither is an argument about either.
  14. It's a YouTube channel.
    The problem
    Not an argument. Where material is published is not a property of whether it is right.
    The version that works
    The books cite real papers with real reference numbers, which anyone can pull. Some support what they are said to support and some carry more weight than the paper claims. Only reading them tells you which, and that reading is the work.

The shape underneath

Ten of fourteen do the same thing.

They attack the wrapper and report it as a refutation of the contents. The catastrophe mythology, the cover-up chapter, the prepping advice, the channel, the author's degree.

Binning a parcel because you recognise the wrapping is fast, and in the wild it is usually right. Which is exactly why it is dangerous here, where the parcel contains claims that can be checked.

The other four are real objections in bad clothes. Dressed properly they are on the next page, and two of them are close to decisive.
