A scan of what exists, not a plan

What funding exists, and what it takes to reach it

Roughly seventy schemes, checked against funder pages on 3 August 2026, for one person: an Australian citizen at Amity on Minjerribah, not incorporated, not attached to a university. Nothing here has been applied for, won or started. It is a map of doors and the locks on them.

Every figure is given in Australian dollars with the source currency alongside. Rates used throughout, all as at 2 August 2026: A$1 = US$0.697, so US$1 is about A$1.43; A$1 = 66.9 Indian rupees, so a crore is about A$149,000; A$1 = 110.67 Japanese yen; A$1 = 0.5186 British pounds; A$1 = 0.6141 euro.

One

Sorted by whether it can be applied for, not by what it pays.

Sorting by prize size produces a list whose top ten entries are all unreachable. The constraint is rarely merit and rarely money. It is entity, nationality and affiliation.

Most United States federal money needs a US-owned entity doing the work inside the US. Most Japanese public research money needs an investigator at a Japanese institution. Most Indian startup money needs an Indian company controlled by resident Indian citizens. University schemes need a university, and cannot be led by an individual however good the idea.

  • Open now. Could be started this month with no change of status. Six entries.
  • One step away. One specific, achievable thing is missing, and the step is named. Twenty entries, five steps.
  • Partner only. Reachable by joining someone else's application, with the partner named. Seventeen entries.
  • Not eligible. Needs an entity, a nationality or an institution that does not exist, or has ended. Listed with the reason so the ground is not covered twice.

An entry paying A$5 million that cannot be applied for is worth less than one paying A$20,000 that can.

What that list actually says

Of six open doors, one is a venture capital pitch, two pay nothing, and one closes in under two weeks with an unresolved eligibility question sitting on it. That is the true width of the opening, and it is worth seeing before the larger numbers below.

Three · one step away

Twenty entries, five steps, and the steps are not the same size.

Grouped by the step rather than the funder, because the grouping is the finding. One step is free and takes a day. One is a relocation decision.

Six

What could not be confirmed, and what has changed name or closed.

A scan that reports only what it found is half a scan. These are the gaps, stated as gaps.

Renames and restructures. Open Philanthropy became Coefficient Giving in November 2025; the old domain returns a permanent redirect, and the change is structural, from a single-donor vehicle to a multi-donor platform. IBM's Call for Code relaunched in 2026 as Call for Code AI, adding shorter Spot Challenges. NASA replaced its annual SBIR solicitation on 17 April 2026 with a rolling announcement to 30 September 2027, folding Ignite in as appendices, so anyone planning around a January solicitation and a summer Ignite round is working from a stale model. CSIRO Kick-Start no longer covers 70 per cent of a project: it is dollar-for-dollar cash matching, and it now needs an ACN rather than an ABN.

Dates that could not be sourced, and were not guessed. Next-round openings for National Science Week 2027, the Queensland Engaging Science Grants, CRC-P Round 20 and ARC Linkage 2027. Whether the Australia-India cyber and critical technology grants will run a Round 6. Whether ROSES-2026 has been released, whether any NOAA space weather competition is funded, and whether NASA iTech exists. Whether SpIN is actively reviewing, given its page still shows 2023 review dates. Whether MeitY's TIDE 2.0, SAMRIDH or XR programmes have a live cohort. The GFDRR Challenge Fund returned a 403 with only pre-2020 rounds surfacing, so it is treated as dormant rather than closed.

Two contradictions worth naming rather than smoothing

The AISRF lead question. One source restricts the Australian lead to a university or publicly funded research agency. Another has a company incorporated in Australia able to lead. Both cannot hold, and which one does decides whether that fund is one step away or partner only. It sits above under one step away with the conflict marked, not resolved by preference.

The Redland eligibility question. The guidelines page returned a 403, so whether an unincorporated individual can hold the one grant here with a live deadline is unknown. That single unanswered question is the difference between a two-week piece of work and a non-starter.

One pattern across the whole scan. On at least three items the opportunity-aggregator sites contradicted the funder's own page, and every time the error ran optimistic. Echoing Green is the clearest: aggregator listings dated July 2026 advertise an open 2027 cohort while the funder's own page says applications are closed. Where they disagreed, the funder was taken as correct.

What it adds up to is narrow. Two entries are actionable this month as things stand, and one of them is a venture capital pitch rather than a grant. The cheapest change of position is an ABN as a sole trader plus public liability insurance, free and about a day's work, which moves two well-fitting schemes from locked to open. Incorporating a company is the larger step and unlocks the larger cluster. Neither has been done, and nothing here has been applied for. The watch list keeps its own record, and what the money would go toward is set out separately.

An evidence ledger for one contested argument. Not a verdict, and not an endorsement: the corrections run in both directions on purpose.
