Watch it yourself

Almost nothing in this argument is hidden. The Sun is photographed continuously, the solar wind is measured on the way in, the magnetic field is mapped by satellite, and the pole position is published by two governments. Anyone with a browser can watch the same instruments the argument is fought over.

The instruments

Where the numbers come from.

These are the primary sources. When a claim gets made in either direction, this is where to go and check it rather than reading someone's summary of it.

  • Space weather, live

    NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center. Solar wind speed and density, geomagnetic storm levels, flare alerts. The official warning service, updated by the minute.

  • The Sun, continuously

    NASA Solar Dynamics Observatory. Full-disc images across many wavelengths. If the outer structure of the Sun ever changed, this is where it would show first.

  • The field and the poles

    NOAA geomagnetism and the World Magnetic Model. Pole positions, field models, and the annual reports that record how fast things are moving.

  • The field from orbit

    ESA Swarm. Three satellites mapping the magnetic field since 2013. The source of most of what is known about the South Atlantic anomaly.

  • The tree-ring record

    IntCal radiocarbon calibration curves. The dataset the Miyake events were found in, and the one a new event would be found in.

  • The star archives

    MAST, holding the Kepler and TESS data. The superflare survey and the 2022 micronova both came out of these archives, and they are open.

  • The site

How to read it

Take the claims seriously enough to check them, which is what taking anything seriously means. Some hold and some do not, and the numbers page marks which is which. Watching daily is genuinely the fastest way to learn how the Sun and the field actually behave, whatever anyone concludes about the deeper argument.

Three

What to actually do.

Almost every answer to this question is written for the wrong event. Look at what the rest of this site documents and the response falls out of the physics rather than out of an emergency management template.

The threat set on these pages is not a long blackout. It is a magnetic field down to a tenth of its strength and staying there for centuries. Particle events an order of magnitude past anything instruments have recorded. A star that superflares roughly once a century across its own class. A solar system already caught with its protective bubble pushed inside the Earth's orbit, leaving the planet sitting in raw interstellar plasma. Torches and tinned food are not a response to that. They are a response to a storm.

What answers that list is mass overhead

Not a technology. A quantity. Rock, soil and water are the only shielding that works against every item at once: hard radiation, a thermal pulse, wind, and anything thrown up that comes back down. It is the same answer arrived at from three directions. It is why Mars habitat designs bury themselves in regolith. It is why Derinkuyu exists. It is what the traditions mean by going under the hill and waiting.

There is even a right depth. A few metres of rock handles the radiation. Going deeper buys thermal stability and volume, but rock gains twenty-five to thirty degrees per kilometre, so past a point you are fighting the planet's own heat. The sweet spot is tens of metres, which is exactly where the old ones were dug.

Which is a question somebody has already answered properly

The full version of this argument is not on this site and should not be duplicated here. The threats, all of them names eighteen of them honestly, from the bushfire that will certainly come back to the rock from the sky that almost certainly will not come soon, and then shows the punchline underneath the lot: the same handful of preparations serves every single one. Energy that islands. Water that is yours. Food and fixing. Knowledge that stays. Shelter that holds. People who have met before.

Which is the move that makes everything on this site optional, and it is worth saying slowly. Nobody has to be convinced about the micronova. Nobody has to accept the excursion timing or anything else contested here. Sort by response instead of by cause and the arguing stops mattering, because the cyclone you will definitely meet already justifies the work.

What this page adds is the next question rather than that one. Six preparations answer a household and a town. The same six, taken to the scale the geological record implies, stop being a kit and become a civilisation. That version is being designed and simulated rather than built, and it is set out below at its honest stage.

What makes it one project rather than four

The unit is deliberate. Thirty-eight cubic metres is specified in cubic metres precisely so it can be multiplied, and the same module drops into a flat, an underground block or a hull with no redesign. That is what turns four ambitions into one stack, and it is why the smallest rung carries the load.

It also means the first move is small and available now. Three towers on a terrace is not a gesture toward a subterranean city. It is the first instance of the cell the city is made from.

And every rung earns its keep before anything goes wrong. A food computer is better and cheaper food this year. A networked neighbourhood is a better place to live on an ordinary Tuesday. Building down is thermally cheap, quiet, and hands the surface back to everything else living on it. None of it waits for a catastrophe to justify itself, which is the whole difference between this and a bunker.

What it does not solve, because a claim this broad has to say

Time. Building at that scale takes decades and nothing on the threat list has agreed to wait. The only honest answer is that the smallest rung has to pay for itself immediately, which it does, so the build is not a bet on finishing.

Each other. Mass overhead is no defence against bad governance inside the walls, against someone deciding to own the place, or against a community that stops being one. Every historical shelter that failed, failed that way rather than structurally. That is why the ownership condition is on the list beside the physical ones and not as a nicety.

Wanting to. None of it matters if people do not want to live in it. Which is the actual design brief, and the reason the answer is a city rather than a bunker.

The grid-hardening conversation still matters and it belongs to states and utilities: transformer stockpiles, generation that can run islanded, standards that assume a thinner shield than the twentieth century assumed. Worth knowing about, not what this page is about.

The structure of warning itself

Every warning system on Earth depends on the damage being slower than the news.

This is worth setting out because it is not obvious, it decides what monitoring can and cannot ever deliver, and it puts a hard floor under the whole enterprise.

Space weather forecasting works for one reason: the Sun sends its message faster than it sends its punch. Light leaves and arrives in about eight minutes and twenty seconds. The cloud of plasma that does the damage to grids and pipelines crawls out at a few hundred to a few thousand kilometres a second and takes the better part of a day or three. That gap is the warning. Everything built at Kiruna, Boulder and elsewhere lives inside it.

Which sets a real limit, and a real design constraint

For a distant source, watching gives no warning about the fast component, because the observation is the arrival. Anything slower does trail behind, but cosmic rays are deflected by galactic magnetic fields on the way, so they come in spread over time and off the straight line, which makes them a poor thing to be waiting for.

The design consequence is the important part. If a whole class of event carries no warning by construction, then preparation cannot be built on alerts for it. It has to be built into the thing itself: equipment that survives without notice, records that survive without power, and arrangements that do not need someone to have been told. Warning is a luxury of slow threats.

On using entanglement to get around this: it does not work, and the reason is a theorem rather than an engineering gap. Entanglement is thoroughly demonstrated and is already in service for key distribution and computing, but measuring one half of an entangled pair sends nothing to the other. The correlation only appears once the two results are compared, and comparing them needs an ordinary channel running at or below light speed. No signal, no warning, no exception, and this is one of the better-tested results in physics rather than an assumption.
