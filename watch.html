Watch it yourself

Almost nothing in this argument is hidden. The Sun is photographed continuously, the solar wind is measured on the way in, the magnetic field is mapped by satellite, and the pole position is published by two governments. Anyone with a browser can watch the same instruments the argument is fought over.

The instruments

Where the numbers come from.

These are the primary sources. When a claim gets made in either direction, this is where to go and check it rather than reading someone's summary of it.

  • Space weather, live

    NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center. Solar wind speed and density, geomagnetic storm levels, flare alerts. The official warning service, updated by the minute.

  • The Sun, continuously

    NASA Solar Dynamics Observatory. Full-disc images across many wavelengths. If the outer structure of the Sun ever changed, this is where it would show first.

  • The field and the poles

    NOAA geomagnetism and the World Magnetic Model. Pole positions, field models, and the annual reports that record how fast things are moving.

  • The field from orbit

    ESA Swarm. Three satellites mapping the magnetic field since 2013. The source of most of what is known about the South Atlantic anomaly.

  • The tree-ring record

    IntCal radiocarbon calibration curves. The dataset the Miyake events were found in, and the one a new event would be found in.

  • The star archives

    MAST, holding the Kepler and TESS data. The superflare survey and the 2022 micronova both came out of these archives, and they are open.

The daily programme

Space Weather News.

The frontier side of this argument is not a set of books that stopped in 2022. It is a daily programme that has been reading the same public instruments every morning since 2011, and it is where the claims get updated, revised and occasionally quietly dropped.

  • The daily show

    Suspicious0bservers on YouTube. A few minutes each morning covering solar activity, the geomagnetic field, seismic activity and new papers.

  • The site

    SpaceWeatherNews.com, and Suspicious0bservers.org for the wider project.

  • "The Disaster Cycle"

    The full documentary. The eight-rung cadence card that gets checked rung by rung here comes from it.

  • The books

    The Next End of the World (2020) and the Observer Supplement 2022/2023, with Weatherman's Guide to the Sun underneath both. Cited throughout with reference numbers, which is what makes them checkable.

How to read it

Take the claims seriously enough to check them, which is what taking anything seriously means. Some hold and some do not, and the numbers page marks which is which. Watching daily is genuinely the fastest way to learn how the Sun and the field actually behave, whatever anyone concludes about the deeper argument.

The part that comes apart

What to actually do.

Whether to harden against a long blackout is a separate question from whether the mechanism is right, and nearly every argument about this runs the two together. Worth pulling apart, because the answer to the second one does not depend on the first.

A Carrington-class storm hitting a modern grid is already a planning assumption for governments, insurers and grid operators. The measured weakening of the field means an ordinary storm now arrives against a thinner shield than it would have in 1900. Everything that would carry a household or a town through a long blackout rests on that alone: spare transformers held locally, water that does not need a pump, food that does not need a truck, and a way of keeping working with the cable cut.

Which way the argument runs

If the deeper claim turns out to be right, the same preparation covers it. If it turns out to be wrong, nothing was wasted, because the case for it never rested on the deeper claim. Whichever way the science lands, the work was worth doing.

The structure of warning itself

Every warning system on Earth depends on the damage being slower than the news.

This is worth setting out because it is not obvious, it decides what monitoring can and cannot ever deliver, and it puts a hard floor under the whole enterprise.

Space weather forecasting works for one reason: the Sun sends its message faster than it sends its punch. Light leaves and arrives in about eight minutes and twenty seconds. The cloud of plasma that does the damage to grids and pipelines crawls out at a few hundred to a few thousand kilometres a second and takes the better part of a day or three. That gap is the warning. Everything built at Kiruna, Boulder and elsewhere lives inside it.

Which sets a real limit, and a real design constraint

For a distant source, watching gives no warning about the fast component, because the observation is the arrival. Anything slower does trail behind, but cosmic rays are deflected by galactic magnetic fields on the way, so they come in spread over time and off the straight line, which makes them a poor thing to be waiting for.

The design consequence is the important part. If a whole class of event carries no warning by construction, then preparation cannot be built on alerts for it. It has to be built into the thing itself: equipment that survives without notice, records that survive without power, and arrangements that do not need someone to have been told. Warning is a luxury of slow threats.

On using entanglement to get around this: it does not work, and the reason is a theorem rather than an engineering gap. Entanglement is thoroughly demonstrated and is already in service for key distribution and computing, but measuring one half of an entangled pair sends nothing to the other. The correlation only appears once the two results are compared, and comparing them needs an ordinary channel running at or below light speed. No signal, no warning, no exception, and this is one of the better-tested results in physics rather than an assumption.
