Link by link

The chain

Six steps from a wave in the galaxy to the ocean leaving its bed. Set out in order, each one labelled for how solid it is, because a chain is worth exactly what its weakest link is worth and nobody is served by leaving that unmarked.

Link 1. There is a wavy magnetic sheet in the galaxy

the setting

The Sun's own version of this is textbook. Its spinning magnetic field trails out into a rippling boundary, and the Earth crosses that ripple every week or so. Each crossing can rattle our magnetic field a little. It is the most ordinary and predictable thing in space weather.

  • The claim → the same shape exists at galaxy scale, and the Solar System crosses it too
    Known Disputed The physics that makes a rippling sheet around a spinning magnetised body is real and scales, and alternating magnetic field directions across the galactic disc have been detected repeatedly. Mainstream astronomy has also found real undulating structure right on the doorstep: the Radcliffe Wave, published in 2020, is a rippling ribbon of star-forming gas some ten thousand light years long threading the Sun's own neighbourhood. What is not established is the crossing schedule, or that a crossing is under way now. The geometry is the strong part of this link. The timetable is the weak part.

Link 2. Passing through something changes the Sun's surroundings

the trigger

Unlike the Sun's own sheet, which the solar wind keeps swept clean, the galactic one is dusty. So a crossing would deliver two things at once: material into the Sun's atmosphere, and a magnetic disturbance to its output. This link splits cleanly into a half that is now settled and a half that is not, and the first pass here ran them together.

  • Measured → the Sun's surroundings change enormously, and recently
    Known Published in Nature Astronomy in June 2024: about two million years ago the Solar System passed through a cold, dense cloud of interstellar gas, and the cloud was thick enough to squeeze the Sun's protective bubble down to roughly a fifth of the Earth's distance from it. That puts the planet outside the bubble entirely, sitting in raw interstellar space. A second likely episode around seven million years ago, at the edge of the Local Bubble. Work is under way to look for the fingerprint of it in lunar soil. Whatever anyone concludes about the rest of the chain, the idea that the Sun sails through a bland, unchanging medium is finished.
  • The claim → and enough material accumulates to build a layer the Sun eventually sheds
    Speculative This half is the weakest link in the chain, and the books say as much: the mechanism by which the galaxy would stunt the Sun's output cannot currently be modelled. There is no calculated accumulation rate, no threshold, no energy budget. Without numbers the claim cannot be wrong in any specific way, which is the same as saying it cannot be tested. Spacecraft already measure the dust environment near the Sun directly, so a worked figure would meet the data quickly and either survive or not. Note what the 2024 result does and does not give: a compressed bubble is not an accumulated shell, and squeezing the heliosphere is not the same as loading the star. The highest-value single piece of work available to anyone defending this argument, and its absence is conspicuous.

Link 3. Stars erupt when their surroundings change

the precedent

The standard picture says a star needs a companion to steal fuel from before it can erupt. If that is strict, the Sun is out, because it is on its own. The argument's case is that the picture loosened.

  • 2020 results → an exploding star with no partner found, a sporadically-fed recurring nova still keeping a regular cycle, simulations showing the donor can be almost any kind of star, and a star that wandered into a gas cloud and erupted
    Known Disputed Each of those results is real and they do loosen the requirement. What they loosen it for is the family of collapsed stars, which is a different family from ours. The move from "environment can trigger an outburst in a dead star" to "environment can trigger one in a living star" is not carried by these papers. It is a reasonable place to go looking. It is not a conclusion they support.

Link 4. A big enough jolt unlocks the crust

how water leaves the sea

The Earth's outer shell sits on hot rock that behaves a bit like very slow toffee. If the grip between the two ever let go, the shell could shift, and shifting is what it takes to slop the oceans over the land.

  • The small version → the length of the day glitches by milliseconds, and those glitches correlate with two things: magnetic hiccups from the core, and strong solar storms
    Known The day-length glitches are measured and the correlations are in the literature. Both suspects reach the layer where rock sits on softer rock. That much is ordinary geophysics.
  • The scaled-up version → one event delivers both jolts at once, hard enough to release the whole shell
    Speculative The bench work behind it is real and unusual: run a current through a pile of olivine crystals, discharge it, and the crystals fly; run it through water in rock and the water climbs against gravity in under a quarter of a second. Interesting effects at bench scale. The distance from a benchtop to a planetary shell is enormous, and nothing bridges it yet except the assertion that bigger versions of small things exist. There is an accepted version of a planet reorienting, it has a different name and a different mechanism, and it is worth knowing which one you are arguing for: the Earth turning over.

Link 5. The shed layer brings the rest

the tidy-up

This is where the argument earns its ambition. One event is meant to leave every kind of debris the record contains.

  • Solid pieces flung out with the shell → the rocks from the sky
    Speculative Elegant, in the sense that it answers the impact problem without needing a separate comet to arrive at exactly the right moment. Untested.
  • Heavy elements made in the burst → the short-lived atoms in the deposits
    Speculative The load-bearing piece, and the one least checked in public. What would settle it is ordinary lab work: measured concentrations in dated layers, against the known background from space dust and cosmic rays.
  • Air pressure dropping under a weakened shield, plus supercooled air and an ice-laden flood → the animals frozen mid-meal
    Speculative A proposed answer to the one part of the record that ordinary cooling cannot explain. Nobody has modelled it.

Link 6. The run-up is visible now

the part you can check

The only link with live instruments on it, which makes it the one worth arguing about hardest.

  • Measured → the field is weakening, the weak patch over the South Atlantic is growing and splitting, and the north magnetic pole moved far enough and fast enough to break the world's navigation model in 2019
    Known All of this is real, from satellites and government agencies, and it is the strongest observational card in the argument.
  • Overstated → "five per cent lost per decade", "down twenty to twenty-five per cent", "the pole is accelerating"
    Unsupported The measured global figures are about five per cent per century and about nine per cent down since 1840, and the pole has been slowing since the late 2010s. The bigger numbers look like a local rate over the South Atlantic read as a global one. This matters more than it looks, because these are the instrument readings the "it is happening now" case rests on. Carrying the correct figures is what makes the rest of the case defensible. See the numbers page.

Reading the chain

Where it holds and where it hangs.

The weak link, named plainly

The second half of link 2. Everything downstream of it is a plausibility argument resting on a step with no numbers attached. Links 1, 4 and 6 have real measured components, and since 2024 so does the first half of link 2. Link 3 is real physics pointed at the wrong family of star. What would have to be built is the loading step and link 5, and until they are, the chain is a sketch of a mechanism rather than a mechanism.

Worth separating from the clock, though, because they are different claims and get treated as one. Nothing in this chain requires a tidy six or twelve thousand year timetable. The books argue that any threshold-reaching change in the surroundings can set a star off, which makes a regular cycle a convenience rather than a requirement. Losing the cadence costs the mechanism nothing, and conceding the cadence is not conceding the mechanism.

That is not the same as saying the sketch is wrong. Plenty of real mechanisms started as sketches and got their numbers later, and some of the results that make this one thinkable are only a few years old. It is a reason for patience. It is not a reason for belief, and the two get run together often.

The objections that land →

A correction to how this whole page is framed

There is not one candidate trigger. There are six.

The chain above is one proposal presented as though it were the only one, which makes the open step look like a void. It is not a void. It is a crowded shortlist with nothing selected off it, and that is a very different problem.

Candidate triggers and their published status
Proposed triggerWhere it actually stands
Nothing external at allThe default, and the best supported. Stars like this one superflare roughly once a century and nobody invokes anything outside the star to explain it. If a large event needs no trigger, the whole search for one is optional. This belongs at the top of the list and is usually left off it entirely.
Crossing the galaxy's magnetic sheetThe chain set out above. The sheet is real. When the Solar System last crossed it, and whether crossing does anything to a star, are both unestablished. No worked coupling.
A change in the interstellar mediumThe crossing itself is mainstream and published. A cold dense cloud compressed the heliosphere inside Earth's orbit about two million years ago. What is unmodelled is any effect on solar output. There is a measurable physical channel though: collapse the bubble and the dust filter goes with it, so what the Sun can pick up changes.
Planetary tidal forcingThis is the one that breaks "there is no model". A tidally synchronised dynamo has been published in mainstream solar physics, with the Venus-Earth-Jupiter alignment period of 11.07 years tracking the eleven-year cycle. Contested, because the forces are very weak and the mechanism is the hard part. But it is a model, in a journal, and it is about external forcing of the Sun.
The Sun's motion about the system's centre of massAn older proposal, correlations claimed since the 1960s, generally not accepted. Weakest of the external candidates and usually the first one raised in argument, which is unfortunate.
Accumulated loading from the mediumThat the Sun gathers material as it travels and something eventually gives. No published model found. It does have a measurable input, since the incoming dust is filtered by the Sun's own magnetic field and that filtering is instrumented, so it is not unfalsifiable in principle.
Known Disputed Speculative

Which restates the open question much more usefully

"There is no mechanism" is the wrong description and this site has used it. There are several proposed mechanisms, one of them published as a working model, and one of them requiring no external trigger at all.

The real gap is narrower and more specific: nothing takes a star at this stage from a large flare to a shed shell. Modulating a dynamo is modelled. Ejecting a shell from an evolved star is observed. Joining those two on a star still burning hydrogen in its core is the step nobody has made.

And the practical consequence is worth stating, because it is the same shape as everything else here. An argument resting on six candidates does not fall when one of them fails. The galactic sheet could be wrong entirely and the question would be untouched, because the strongest candidate on the list asks nothing of the galaxy at all.
