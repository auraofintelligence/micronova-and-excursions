# 03. The excursion record, rung by rung

*The cadence list against the palaeomagnetic literature. Checked one rung at a time, because that is where the argument is actually won or lost, and because nobody on either side seems to do it.*

---

## The list being tested

From the "Disaster Cycle" presentation card, the eight-rung version:

```
48,000 y.a.  LASCHAMP 1
42,000 y.a.  LASCHAMP 2
36,000 y.a.  MONO LAKE
30,000 y.a.  MICHOACAN
24,000 y.a.  LAKE MUNGO
18,000 y.a.  HILINA PALI
12,000 y.a.  GOTHENBURG
 6,000 y.a.  NOAH EVENT
             HAPPENING AGAIN NOW
```

Even spacing, six thousand years a rung. The evenness is the argument: a clean beat implies a driver. So the beat is what has to be checked.

## Rung by rung

### Laschamps (~41 ka): **established**

The strongest rung by a distance. Found in volcanic rocks and sediments on every continent, reproduced in global field models rather than resting on any one site, and the first excursion ever recognised. Field strength fell to a small fraction of present during the minimum. It is the reference case, and it is not in doubt.

*On the list as two rungs, 48 ka and 42 ka.* The literature treats Laschamps as one event centred near 41 ka. Splitting it into two events 6,000 years apart is a claim beyond the published record; if the split is real it needs its own evidence, and the reason to look for it should not be that the ladder needs a rung there.

### Mono Lake (~34.5 ka): **established**

Well documented and recovered far from its type locality, including in Drake Passage sediments (2025). The date sits near 34.5 ka, not 36 ka, which is inside reasonable dating spread.

### Michoacan (~30 ka): **not established as an excursion**

This one does not appear in the standard excursion inventories. What exists in the Mexican literature is a palaeosecular variation record from the Michoacán-Guanajuato monogenetic volcanic field and the Chichinautzin Formation, covering the last 30,000 years from radiocarbon-dated lava flows. That study reports the virtual dipole moment increasing gradually over the period with a peak near 9 ka. It is a secular variation study, not a claim of a global excursion.

**Verdict: the name is carrying more than the record does.** If there is a formal Michoacán excursion in the literature, it has not surfaced in the standard reviews, and the burden sits with whoever put it on the ladder.

### Lake Mungo (~30 ka): **real record, wrong rung, contested meaning**

Barbetti and McElhinny (1976) is a genuine and much-cited study: archaeomagnetic directions from Aboriginal fireplaces and ovenstones along the ancient shoreline in south-eastern Australia, showing departures up to 120 degrees from the axial dipole direction, with intensity first rising to about three times present and later falling to about a third.

Two problems for the ladder:

1. **The published age is about 30,000 years, not 24,000.** The list has Lake Mungo at 24 ka and an unnamed or Michoacan rung at 30 ka. The original work puts Mungo at the 30 ka slot. Two rungs are competing for one record.
2. **Whether it was a global field event is not settled.** Follow-up work modelled it as a local core source (an eccentric radial dipole, current loops), which is a way of saying the signal may be regional. Later reviews have questioned whether the anomalies originally assigned to Mungo reflect the global field at all.

It is a real palaeomagnetic anomaly. It is not a confirmed global excursion, and its date does not fit where the ladder puts it.

### Hilina Pali (~18.5 ka): **not supported**

Reviewed by Liu et al. (2018): no directional evidence and no palaeointensity evidence for a Hilina Pali excursion. Standard reviews list it with the postulated excursions that did not survive checking, alongside Tianshui (~10.2 ka) and Rockall (~26 ka).

**Verdict: this rung is empty.**

### Gothenburg (~12 ka): **not supported**

Proposed by Mörner in the 1970s and not sustained. It is the rung the whole argument most needs, because it is the one that pairs with the Younger Dryas, the megafaunal collapse and the last claimed micronova. It is also among the weakest.

**Verdict: this rung is empty, and it is the load-bearing one.**

### The "Noah event" (~6 ka): **not an excursion claim, and stated as such**

The 2022 Supplement is open that the ~6 ka event is absent from the official Heinrich list, and argues it is invisible because it fell in an interglacial rather than a glacial. The supporting evidence offered is circumstantial: the greenest Green Sahara epoch at 6 ka, volcanic evidence at 6 ka, and a Holocene geomagnetic dip whose datasets scatter between 4.5 ka and 7.5 ka and average near 6 ka.

**Verdict: an inference, presented as one.** Averaging datasets that disagree by three thousand years, and then treating the average as a dated event, is the weak step. It is not fabrication, and the book does not hide the scatter.

## Score

| Rung | Status |
|---|---|
| Laschamps 1 (48 ka) | not in the record as a separate event |
| Laschamps 2 (42 ka) | **established** (as the single Laschamps, ~41 ka) |
| Mono Lake (36 ka) | **established** (~34.5 ka) |
| Michoacan (30 ka) | not established |
| Lake Mungo (24 ka) | real regional record, published at ~30 ka, global status contested |
| Hilina Pali (18 ka) | not supported |
| Gothenburg (12 ka) | not supported |
| Noah (6 ka) | inferred, not an excursion |

**Two rungs of eight hold.** The two that hold are the two the whole field agrees on, and they sit about 6,500 years apart, which is the one genuinely interesting number on the board.

## What this does and does not do to the argument

**It does not falsify the micronova.** An empty rung means "not demonstrated", not "demonstrated absent". Palaeomagnetic and radiocarbon dating carry real uncertainty that grows with depth, and absence of a recovered signal in a sparse record is weak evidence of absence.

**It does gut the cadence as evidence.** The regular beat is the argument's most persuasive feature and the reason the presentation card works: eight evenly spaced rungs read as a metronome. If six of the eight are unconfirmed, unsupported, or misdated, then the evenness is a property of the list, not a property of the record. **A ladder built to a rhythm cannot then be offered as proof of the rhythm.** That is the circularity, and it is the single most damaging thing an informed sceptic can say. It should be conceded before it is said.

**And it points at the honest version of the work.** The two confirmed excursions sit roughly 6,500 years apart. Two points do not make a cycle. Three would be interesting. The right response to this table is not to defend the ladder; it is to go and see whether the 30 ka and 18 ka and 12 ka slots hold anything at all, with the dating done blind to what the answer is supposed to be. That is a real research programme, and it is more interesting than the argument it would settle.
