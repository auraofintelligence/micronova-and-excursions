Layer 4 · the clock

Eight rungs, checked

The ladder is the most persuasive thing in the whole argument. Eight collapses of the Earth's magnetic field, six thousand years apart, all the way down. Even spacing reads as a metronome, and a metronome implies something keeping time. So the spacing is the thing to check, and checking it one rung at a time is work almost nobody on either side has done in public.

The list being tested

What the card says.

From the "Disaster Cycle" presentation. Read down and the rhythm does the arguing: forty-eight, forty-two, thirty-six, thirty, twenty-four, eighteen, twelve, six, now. What follows is each of those against the palaeomagnetic literature, in order.

  1. 48,000 y.a. Laschamp 1 Unsupported No excursion is proposed at this age by anyone. The nearest events either side are Laschamp at about 41,000 years and the Norwegian-Greenland Sea at about 60,000 to 64,000. There is a real dating argument inside the Laschamp type locality, because the Olby lava flow has been dated near 37,000 years against Laschamps flows at 41,900, but that is a dispute about how long one event lasted, not evidence of a second one six thousand years earlier.
  2. 42,000 y.a. Laschamp 2 Known The strongest rung by a distance, as the single Laschamp event dated to between 42,200 and 41,500 years ago. Found in lava and sediment on every continent, reproduced in global field models, and the first excursion ever recognised. The field fell by as much as 90 per cent. Not in doubt, and it is the tie point everyone else dates against.
  3. 36,000 y.a. Mono Lake Known Disputed In the accepted inventory, one of the five most studied excursions of the last 780,000 years, and recovered far from where it was named, including Drake Passage sediments in 2025. It sits nearer 34,000 years than 36,000. The live argument is about its own type locality: one line of work has argued the record in the Wilson Creek beds is actually Laschamp misdated, and the ages there run anywhere from 30,000 to 41,000 depending on the method. The excursion is accepted. Where it is written down is still being fought over.
  4. 30,000 y.a. Michoacan Unsupported No excursion of this name appears anywhere in the palaeomagnetic literature, confirmed or postulated. Mexican volcanic rocks have been used to propose excursions, but at around a million years old, and the studies covering the last 30,000 years there are records of ordinary field wandering. If there is a source for the name, it has not surfaced, and the burden sits with whoever put it on the ladder.
  5. 24,000 y.a. Lake Mungo Disputed A real and much-cited study: magnetic directions from Aboriginal fireplaces and oven stones along the old shoreline in south-western New South Wales, showing departures of up to 120 degrees. The 1976 work found two separate episodes, a main one and a second around 26,000 radiocarbon years with the field strength dropping sharply, and it is the second that the standard reviews carry. So the rung is placed about where the literature puts it. What it does not have is confirmation: follow-up work modelled it as a local source in the Earth's core, and it sits on the list of postulated excursions that have not been accepted into the inventory.
  6. 18,000 y.a. Hilina Pali Disputed The most alive of the unconfirmed rungs, and an argument still running. In 2018 reversed directions turned up in Ukrainian loess dated to about 18,000 years, published as a possible Hilina Pali record, and it drew a formal published Comment accusing it of reinforcement syndrome, which is the habit of finding an excursion because you went looking for one, plus a Reply. In the same year a Black Sea record found no sign of it. A 2025 Drake Passage study also found nothing, and said in the same breath that the excursion may have been regional, or too short for those sediments to catch. Lake cores in the Polar Urals recorded a distinct event near 20,000 years and are being worked on now. Contested is the correct word. Empty is not.
  7. 12,000 y.a. Gothenburg Disputed Proposed from southern Scandinavia in 1977, lasting perhaps a thousand years, with striking changes in the direction the field pointed. Since then it has been claimed in Barents Sea sediments and used as a dating marker in the Central Russian Upland, and it has never been accepted into the inventory. It is the rung the argument needs most, because it is the one that pairs with the Younger Dryas cold snap, the megafauna collapse and the last claimed micronova. It is also the one that has been sitting unconfirmed the longest.
  8. 6,000 y.a. The "Noah" event Speculative Offered as an inference, and stated as one. The supporting evidence is circumstantial: the greenest of the green Sahara periods at 6,000 years, volcanic evidence at 6,000 years, and a dip in field strength whose datasets scatter between 4,500 and 7,500 years and average near 6,000. Averaging datasets that disagree by three thousand years and then treating the average as a dated event is the weak step. No excursion is claimed here by anyone, including the people who put the rung on the ladder.
  9. Now Claimed to be under way Disputed The field is weakening and the poles are moving, both measured. Whether that is the start of an excursion or an ordinary episode in a restless field is the live question, and the honest answer is that nobody can tell yet from inside it.

The score

Two accepted, three postulated, two absent.

That is a more useful result than a simple pass or fail, and it is not the result either side usually reports. Most of the rungs are not invented. They are real proposed excursions, named in the literature by working palaeomagnetists, sitting on a published list of events that have been put forward and not yet accepted. The dispute on those rungs is confirmation, not existence.

Where the unconfirmed ones live

A 2020 review lists the excursions proposed for the last 30,000 years that have not made it into the accepted inventory. Gothenburg at about 12,000 years, Hilina Pali at about 19,000, Lake Mungo at about 26,000, and Rockall also at about 26,000, along with three others. The review's own reason for holding them out is worth quoting fairly: some of these records have been traced to remagnetised sediment, disturbed layers, changes in the material being deposited, and what the field calls reinforcement syndrome, which is the habit of finding an excursion because you already expected one there.

What this does

And what it does not.

It does not falsify anything

Unconfirmed means not demonstrated, which is a different thing from demonstrated absent. Dating in mud and lava carries real uncertainty that grows the further back you go, and the record is patchy because it depends on what happened to preserve and what happened to get sampled. The 2025 study that found no Hilina Pali in Drake Passage sediments said so itself: the event may have been regional, or too short for those sediments to catch.

But it does take the metronome away

Not because the rungs are empty. Because of what sits between them. Count every excursion proposed for the last 30,000 years, including the unconfirmed ones the ladder relies on, and they land at roughly 2,700 years, 10,000, 12,000, 13,000, 19,000, 26,000 and 26,000. That is not a beat. It is a scatter, with two events almost on top of each other and a long gap where the ladder wants a rung. You cannot accept the unconfirmed events that fill the ladder and refuse the unconfirmed events that break it. Whichever standard of proof you pick, apply it to all of them.

The right answer to this table is not to defend the ladder. It is to go and find out what the unconfirmed rungs actually hold, with the dating done blind to what the answer is meant to be.

Three of them are live research right now rather than closed questions, and one of those, Hilina Pali, has people publishing against each other in the same journals this decade. That is a real piece of work, it is ordinary palaeomagnetism rather than anything exotic, and it is more interesting than the argument it would settle. What it would take is set out in what would settle it.

An evidence ledger for one contested argument. Not a verdict, and not an endorsement: the corrections run in both directions on purpose.
