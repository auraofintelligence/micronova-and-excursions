Layer 4 · the clock

Eight rungs, checked

The ladder is the most persuasive thing in the whole argument. Eight collapses of the Earth's magnetic field, six thousand years apart, all the way down. Even spacing reads as a metronome, and a metronome implies something keeping time. So the spacing is the thing to check, and checking it one rung at a time is work almost nobody on either side has done in public.

The list being tested

What the card says.

From the "Disaster Cycle" presentation. Read down and the rhythm does the arguing: forty-eight, forty-two, thirty-six, thirty, twenty-four, eighteen, twelve, six, now. What follows is each of those against the palaeomagnetic literature, in order.

  1. 48,000 y.a. Laschamp 1 Unsupported The literature treats Laschamps as one event near 41,000 years, not two events six thousand years apart. If a split is real it needs its own evidence, and the reason to go looking should not be that the ladder has a gap there.
  2. 42,000 y.a. Laschamp 2 Known The strongest rung by a distance, as the single Laschamps event at about 41,000 years. Found in lava and sediment on every continent, reproduced in global field models, the first excursion ever recognised. Field strength fell to a small fraction of today's. Not in doubt.
  3. 36,000 y.a. Mono Lake Known Well documented and recovered far from where it was named, including in Drake Passage sediments in 2025. Sits closer to 34,500 years than 36,000, which is inside reasonable dating spread.
  4. 30,000 y.a. Michoacan Unsupported Does not appear in the standard excursion inventories. What exists in the Mexican literature is a study of ordinary field wandering from radiocarbon-dated lava flows in the Michoacán-Guanajuato field, covering the last 30,000 years. It reports the field's strength rising gradually over that time, peaking near 9,000 years ago. That is not a claim of an excursion. The name is carrying more than the record does.
  5. 24,000 y.a. Lake Mungo Disputed A real and much-cited study: magnetic directions from Aboriginal fireplaces and oven stones along the old shoreline in south-western New South Wales, showing departures of up to 120 degrees. Two problems. Its published age is about 30,000 years, not 24,000, so two rungs are competing for one record. And follow-up work modelled it as a local source in the Earth's core, which is a way of saying the signal may be regional rather than global.
  6. 18,000 y.a. Hilina Pali Unsupported Reviewed in 2018: no directional evidence and no field-strength evidence. It sits in the standard reviews alongside the other proposed excursions that did not survive checking. This rung is empty.
  7. 12,000 y.a. Gothenburg Unsupported Proposed in the 1970s and not sustained since. It is the rung the argument needs most, because it is the one that pairs with the Younger Dryas cold snap, the megafauna collapse and the last claimed micronova. It is also among the weakest.
  8. 6,000 y.a. The "Noah" event Speculative Offered as an inference, and stated as one. The supporting evidence is circumstantial: the greenest of the green Sahara periods at 6,000 years, volcanic evidence at 6,000 years, and a dip in field strength whose datasets scatter between 4,500 and 7,500 years and average near 6,000. Averaging datasets that disagree by three thousand years and then treating the average as a dated event is the weak step.
  9. Now Claimed to be under way Disputed The field is weakening and the poles are moving, both measured. Whether that is the start of an excursion or an ordinary episode in a restless field is the live question, and the honest answer is that nobody can tell yet from inside it.

The score

Two of eight.

The two that hold are the two the whole field agrees on, Laschamps and Mono Lake, and they sit about 6,500 years apart. That is the one genuinely interesting number on the board, and it is two points. Two points do not make a rhythm. Three would start to.

2Rungs that hold as established global excursions
3Rungs with no support in the literature
2Rungs that are real records but disputed, misdated, or both
1Rung that is an inference from proxies, not a dated event

What this does

And what it does not.

It does not falsify anything

An empty rung means not demonstrated, which is a different thing from demonstrated absent. Dating in mud and lava carries real uncertainty that grows the further back you go, and the record is sparse because it depends on what happened to preserve and what happened to get sampled. Failing to find a signal in a patchy record is weak evidence that there was nothing there.

It does gut the clock as evidence

The regular beat is the argument's best feature and the reason the card works. If six of the eight rungs are unconfirmed, unsupported or misdated, then the evenness is a property of the list rather than a property of the rock. A ladder assembled to a rhythm cannot then be offered as proof of the rhythm. That is the circle, and it is the most damaging single thing an informed sceptic can say. Better to concede it first than to have it said to you.

The right answer to this table is not to defend the ladder. It is to go and find out whether the empty rungs hold anything at all, with the dating done blind to what the answer is meant to be.

That is a real piece of work, it is ordinary palaeomagnetism rather than anything exotic, and it is more interesting than the argument it would settle. What it would take is set out in what would settle it.

An evidence ledger for one contested argument. Not a verdict, and not an endorsement: the corrections run in both directions on purpose.
