What the evidence is for

What is being built

The aim is one build that answers the whole threat list at once. What makes that possible is sorting by response rather than by cause, which is what turns eighteen arguments into six things worth having. Nobody has to be convinced about the micronova to have a use for it.

One

Sorted by response, not by cause.

Eighteen threats are set out at Grain by Grain, with the six preparations that serve every one. The threats have almost nothing in common. The preparations are the same six.

Volcanic winter, a pandemic, a bad harvest, an undersea cable cut, a transformer bank lost to a geomagnetic storm: the causes have nothing in common and the arguments about their odds run in different directions. What the household in the middle of one experiences is close to identical. Power gone. Water not arriving. Food thinner than the shelf suggests. The person who knew how to fix the thing unreachable.

Two · the unit cell

A closed-loop food system in 38 cubic metres.

A twenty-foot shipping container holds about 33 cubic metres, so this is a container and a bit. It is specified as a volume and nothing else, which is the part that does the work later.

Inside it: hydroponic and aeroponic towers, insects, poultry, fish, microgreens, mushrooms and root crops, sized against each other rather than chosen separately. Fish water is the nutrient supply for the towers. Trimmings and kitchen waste go to the insects. The insects feed the poultry and the fish. Spent substrate goes to the mushrooms, and the root crops are the part that keeps without power.

Each part eats another part's waste, so the outputs that would be a disposal problem separately are the inputs that close the loop.

The reason it is specified in cubic metres rather than square metres of floor is that a volume carries no site assumption inside it. The same module drops into a flat, into an underground block, or into a hull, with no redesign.

The first phase is three towers and 120 pots on a terrace. Small, and available now: not a gesture toward a city, the first instance of the cell the city is made from.

Three · down

Subterranean cities, sited in sand and aquifer.

The allowance is about 100 cubic metres of dwelling per person: roughly a 40 square metre flat each at normal ceiling height. The full write-up sits at subterranean cities.

Sand and aquifer is the siting, chosen for one reason with three payoffs. Moving groundwater is a heat sink, and a heat sink is what makes a sealed volume survivable: a space with people and machines in it heats up, and something has to carry that heat away. The same water is the supply. The same water and overburden are the shielding. One site condition, three problems answered.

The excavation is designed to pay for itself rather than sit as a cost line. Minjerribah sand carries zircon, rutile and ilmenite, which come out as product, and part of the remaining silica goes back in as structural liner. The spoil is the building, so the two largest numbers in a project like this, digging and materials, are set against each other instead of added. The surface is left to recover above.

The density only sounds impossible in two dimensions.

Twenty million people on 275 square kilometres is about 73,000 per square kilometre, denser than any city on Earth and the number that ends the conversation. Spread across thirty-odd levels it is about 2,400 per square kilometre per level, which is ordinary Australian suburb. A level is the unit a person lives on.

What that arithmetic is and is not

The volumes are arithmetic and anyone can redo them in a minute. They establish that the space exists, which is the objection usually raised first. They establish nothing about geotechnics, water table behaviour under a city, ventilation at that scale, cost, or whether anybody wants to live there.

Four · why mass rather than alerts

Warning depends on the damage being slower than the news.

Space weather forecasting works because the Sun sends its message faster than it sends its punch. Not every arrival is built that way, and the ones that are not decide the shape of the shelter.

Light from a flare arrives in about eight minutes and twenty seconds. The plasma cloud that does the damage to grids and pipelines crawls out at a few hundred to a few thousand kilometres a second and takes the better part of a day or three. That gap is the warning, and everything built at Kiruna and Boulder lives inside it.

For the fast half there is no gap. X-rays and gamma rays arrive with the picture of the event that made them, so the flash and the news are one signal, and radio blackout begins as the flare becomes visible. Energetic protons trail by tens of minutes, which is enough to move a crew and not much else. Anything travelling at light speed from the galactic centre has the same property stretched across 26,000 years, and no instrument changes it.

So preparation against the fast class cannot rest on alerts. It has to sit in the thing itself: equipment that keeps working without notice, records that survive without power, arrangements that do not need anyone to have been told. Mass overhead at tens of metres is the answer that covers the list at once, because it is already in place before the signal and it does not ask which cause arrived.

Five · up

A solar sensor swarm, because one point is not a picture.

A decentralised network for continuous multi-angle, full-spectrum observation of the objects ranked most significant in the solar system. Designed, not flying.

Parker Solar Probe is one point sampling a plasma that is structured in space and varying in time. When its instruments record a change, there is no way to separate whether the spacecraft flew through a structure or whether something changed while it sat there. That is geometry rather than budget.

Four points define a tetrahedron, the smallest arrangement that resolves a gradient in three dimensions, which is why ESA flies four Cluster spacecraft and NASA flies four MMS spacecraft in formation. The principle is orthodox. What is missing from the public record is a multi-point formation inside the corona. So the geometry is known and the fleet is speculative: a design with no hardware, no costed launch path and no agency behind it. The instruments already flying are listed on the watch page.

Six · the pattern

Define the cell. Own it. Replicate.

The same three-step move runs at every scale here, which is why the parts fit together rather than merely appearing in the same document.

Nothing in the stack requires a single owner, a single funder or a single decision, because the cell is the thing that gets built and the cell is small.

Knowledge is the cheapest of the six preparations and the best evidenced. Simeulue is the measure of it: seven dead out of roughly seventy thousand in 2004, on an instruction carried for ninety-seven years with no institution, siren or funding behind it. The arithmetic that puts a price on that sits on the earthquake page.

The honest state of it

Designed and being simulated: the food cell, the underground city, the swarm. At first-phase scale: three towers and 120 pots. Nothing is dug and nothing is flying. The gap between those two lists is where the work is.

Read as a reason for dread, this material produces bunkers, which help one household once. Read as a design constraint, it produces local food, local water, spread skills, kept records and neighbours who know each other, all of which make an ordinary decade better and happen to carry a bad one.
