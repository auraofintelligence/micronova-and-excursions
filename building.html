What the evidence is for

What is being built

The aim is one civilisational upgrade that answers the eighteen threats set out at Grain by Grain in a single build. What makes that possible is sorting by response rather than by cause. Threats argued one at a time get argued forever, and whoever is more patient wins each round, so nothing gets built. Sorted by response, the arguing becomes optional: the list of things worth having comes out short, and the same short list serves the lot. Nobody has to be convinced about the micronova to have a use for it. What follows is designed and being simulated, with one small piece at first-phase scale.

One

Sorted by response, not by cause.

Eighteen threats are set out at Grain by Grain, with the six preparations that serve every one. The threats have almost nothing in common. The preparations are the same six.

Volcanic winter, a pandemic, a bad harvest, an undersea cable cut, a transformer bank lost to a geomagnetic storm: the causes have nothing in common and the arguments about their odds run in different directions. What the household in the middle of one experiences is closer to identical. Power gone. Water not arriving. Food thinner than the shelf suggests. The person who knew how to fix the thing unreachable.

Two · the unit cell

A closed-loop food system in 38 cubic metres.

A twenty-foot shipping container holds about 33 cubic metres, so this is a container and a bit. It is specified as a volume and nothing else, which is the part that does the work later.

Insects9 m³Eat plant and kitchen waste. Feed the poultry and the fish.

Each part eats another part's waste, so the outputs that would be a disposal problem separately are the inputs that close the loop.

Conditions are run by software: light, temperature, humidity, nutrient balance, feeding schedules. The crop mix is a setting rather than a fixture, so a household short on protein and one short on greens run the same hardware on different ratios. That part is designed and being simulated, with no season behind it yet.

The reason it is specified in cubic metres rather than square metres of floor is that a volume carries no site assumption inside it. The same module drops into a flat, into an underground block, or into a hull, with no redesign. That is the join that turns several separate ambitions into one stack instead of a wish list.

The first phase is three towers and 120 pots on a terrace. Small, and available now: not a gesture toward a city, the first instance of the cell the city is made from.

Three · down

Subterranean cities, sited in sand and aquifer.

The allowance is about 100 cubic metres of dwelling per person: roughly a 40 square metre flat each at normal ceiling height. The full write-up sits at subterranean cities.

For scale: living space per person in Hong Kong is commonly reported near 16 square metres, and habitable volume aboard the International Space Station works out in the sixty to seventy cubic metre range per crew member for a crew of six. The underground figure is not a survival ration. It is a flat.

Sand and aquifer is the siting, chosen for one reason with three payoffs. Moving groundwater is a heat sink, and a heat sink is what makes a sealed volume survivable: a space with people and machines in it heats up, and something has to carry that heat away. The same water is the supply. The same water and overburden are the shielding. One site condition, three problems answered.

The excavation is designed to pay for itself rather than sit as a cost line. Minjerribah sand carries zircon, rutile and ilmenite, which come out as product, and part of the remaining silica goes back in as structural liner. The spoil is the building, so the two largest numbers in a project like this, digging and materials, are set against each other instead of added. The surface is left to recover above.

The density only sounds impossible in two dimensions.

Twenty million people on 275 square kilometres is about 73,000 per square kilometre, which is denser than any city on Earth and is the number that ends the conversation. Spread across thirty-odd levels it is about 2,400 per square kilometre per level, which is ordinary Australian suburb. A level is the unit a person lives on.

What that arithmetic is and is not

The volumes are arithmetic and anyone can redo them in a minute. They establish that the space exists, which is the objection usually raised first. They establish nothing about geotechnics, water table behaviour under a city, ventilation at that scale, cost, or whether anybody wants to live there.

Four · up

A virtual solar swarm, because one point is not a picture.

A persistent decentralised sensor network for continuous multi-angle, full-spectrum observation of the 200 objects ranked most significant in the solar system. Designed, not flying.

The argument for it is categorical rather than incremental. Parker Solar Probe is one point sampling a plasma that is structured in space and varying in time. When its instruments record a change, there is no way to separate whether the spacecraft flew through a structure or whether something changed while it sat there. That is geometry, not budget. Parker could fly another century and the ambiguity would still be there on the last orbit.

Four is not an arbitrary minimum for the baseline picket. Four points define a tetrahedron, the smallest arrangement that resolves a gradient in three dimensions, which is why ESA flies four Cluster spacecraft and NASA flies four MMS spacecraft in formation. The principle is orthodox. What is missing from the public record is a multi-point formation inside the corona.

Five · the arena

The Sensorium: one pipeline, every hypothesis.

A shared arena for open hypothesis testing, designed so mainstream and contested ideas go through the same five steps and come out comparable.

The steps are plain: state the hypothesis so it can fail, identify the parameters it depends on, implement the model, run the simulation, compare the output against observation. A hypothesis that will not survive that treatment is not excluded by anybody's judgement, it just does not produce a run. Nothing is filtered at the door for where it came from, because the pipeline is cheaper than the gatekeeping.

This repository is a small worked example of that pipeline running by hand. Three runnable calculations sit in tools/, each written during an argument on this site rather than after it. One tests whether the excursion dates carry a clean cycle or a scatter. One bounds how much mass the Sun can have shed before the planets' orbits forbid it. One prices the geometry of Earth being in the firing line.

Two of the three came back partly against the case they were built for

That is the only evidence available that the pipeline is doing anything. A test that only ever agrees with the person who wrote it is a decoration. The runs are in the repository and the method is in each file header. What would settle more of it is listed on the page of things worth checking.

Six · the people

Who does the work.

This is the part usually missing from documents at this scale, and it is the part that decides whether any of the rest is arithmetic or fantasy.

The design work is not done by a funded engineering team. It is done by students. In 2019 the author helped open a student innovation lab in Kolhapur, Maharashtra, alongside a former ISRO mission director, a former DRDO scientist and a nuclear physicist. It took 200 students in its first year across 12 projects in aerospace, space robotics and automation, with a student rocket launching centre beside it.

India has the largest population aged 5 to 24 of any country, commonly put around half a billion, which is more young people than the whole population of the European Union. Given that pipeline and AI tooling, swarm design starts to look like coursework rather than a contract: a formation geometry problem set, a thermal budget, a comms topology, done by people who will be alive when it flies.

Seven · locally

Twenty-eight doors on one island.

The community ledger holds twenty-eight live public project pages for Minjerribah. It is a menu rather than a portfolio.

A portfolio says here is what was done. A menu says here is a page that already exists, and whoever walks in decides what it becomes. Ferry data. A night market. A legal memory workbench. Disaster kiosks. A film festival. Mineral moonshots. The spread is the design: whoever cares about tides, or court records, or a Saturday market, lands on a page with something already on it rather than a blank form asking for a proposal.

The pages are live and the projects are entry points, not finished work. Some are a page and a plan. The film side connects to the Infinity Engine, where the music and visuals pipeline lives, and the song catalogue sits at the music universe. Whether a card turns into anything depends on someone picking it up.

Eight · the pattern

Define the cell. Own it. Replicate.

The same three-step move runs at every scale on this page, which is why the parts fit together rather than merely appearing in the same document.

Nothing in the stack requires a single owner, a single funder or a single decision, because the cell is the thing that gets built and the cell is small.

The evidence pages sit downstream of that. They argue about how much time there is, what the shelter has to hold against, and how much of the record is settled. Those arguments change the specification. They do not change whether it is worth having, because the six preparations pay for themselves in ordinary years as well as extraordinary ones. The political version of the argument is at P4A.

The honest state of it

Designed and being simulated: the food cell, the underground city, the swarm, the arena. At first-phase scale: three towers and 120 pots. Live and public: twenty-eight project pages and the calculations in this repository. Nothing is dug and nothing is flying. The gap between those lists is where the work is.
