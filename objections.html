The sceptic's best case

What actually lands

If you are going to hold this position, hold it against these, because the cheap dismissals are not what will beat you. Setting these out is the price of setting out the cheap ones: a ledger that catalogues bad objections and hides the good ones is doing advocacy, not thinking.

1. The clock may be an artefact of the list

the hardest one
  • The objection → the rungs were chosen by people who already held the rhythm, from a record with dating uncertainty in the hundreds to thousands of years, and where a rung was empty a candidate was found for it
    Disputed Under those conditions a regular spacing is close to guaranteed whether or not anything is keeping time. The check partly bears it out and partly does not. Two rungs have nothing behind them at any status, and Laschamp is split into two events with no evidence of a second one. But three of the rungs are real published proposals that simply have never been confirmed, so the harsh version of this objection overreaches: most of the ladder is not invented. Damage: real, and it survives in a sharper form. Take every excursion proposed for the last 30,000 years, including the unconfirmed ones the ladder relies on, and they land at roughly 2,700 years, 10,000, 12,000, 13,000, 19,000, 26,000 and 26,000. That is a scatter, not a beat. You cannot accept the unconfirmed events that fill the ladder and reject the unconfirmed events that break it. The honest position is to stop offering the ladder as proof of its own rhythm while still holding the question open.

2. The mechanism has no model

the other hard one
  • The objection → every link past the setting is a plausibility argument with no numbers: no accumulation rate, no threshold, no energy budget, no predicted signature
    Speculative Without numbers a claim cannot be wrong in any specific way, which is the same as saying it cannot be tested. A figure for how much material would have to arrive, and how fast, would meet spacecraft measurements of the dust near the Sun within a week and either survive or die. The gap is not small. It is the difference between a hypothesis and a story. The strongest available reply: new mechanisms often start as plausibility arguments and get their numbers later, and some of the enabling results here are only a few years old. Fair as an argument for patience. Not an argument for present belief, and that is where the frontier most often overreaches.

3. The specific numbers do not survive checking

the avoidable one
  • The objection → "five per cent per decade" against a measured five per cent per century, "down twenty to twenty-five per cent" against a measured nine per cent, continued pole acceleration against a measured slowdown
    Unsupported A wrong number does not falsify a hypothesis. These particular numbers matter because they are the observational core of the "it is happening now" case. If the field is declining at five per cent per century and the pole is slowing, the urgency argument has lost its instrument reading. The strongest available reply is genuinely good: the global average is not the whole story, the South Atlantic weakens far faster locally, and particle penetration has deepened. Which also means the global figures should never have been quoted the way they were.

4. The forecast window has moved

the method one
  • The objection → the 2020 book put grid-threatening field loss around 2030 to 2040 and a reversal moment at 2040 to 2060; the 2022 supplement has the event "this century"
    Disputed This is the strongest case available for the pseudoscience charge, and unlike the version in the catalogue it is about method rather than the person. A forecast that survives every observation by widening has stopped doing work. The strongest available reply: the books do state the timing as speculative in their own words and give rival cycle lengths rather than one confident figure. Hedged forecasts are legitimate. Presenting the hedged version in the text and the confident version on the card is not.

5. The wrapper is not costless

the one that gets avoided
  • The objection → the cover-up chapter, the mission patch read as the four horsemen, a passing star identified with Nibiru, Atlantis, the Texas rock wall
    Disputed This is not guilt by association. It is a question about consistent standards. If the same author applies one level of rigour to journal citations and another to a mission logo, a reader has no way to tell which mode a given page is written in. That is a real cost and it is paid by the reader. The strongest available reply: books can mix registers, and the physical claims carry citations that can be checked independently of the mythological ones. True, and it puts the whole burden of separating them on whoever is reading. Which is the honest reason a ledger like this needs to exist at all.

6. Coincidence-counting cuts both ways

the structural one
  • The objection → "too many coincidences to ignore" is a real form of reasoning and also the standard engine of conspiracy thinking; without a count of the coincidences looked for and not found, there is no denominator
    Disputed Nobody lists the planets that did not change, or the nearby stars that did not flare. Three stars are offered as a sequence pointing at the Sun. The number of stars searched, across decades of improving instruments that would turn up new variability anywhere they were aimed, is not given. The strongest available reply, and it is a good one: in at least one case the prediction came first. A Harvard-Smithsonian critic asked whether nearby stars towards the galactic centre should be reacting, and examples were then found. Predicting in advance is worth more than fitting afterwards. It would be worth a great deal more with the negative cases reported too.

After all six

What survives.

Not the mechanism. Not the clock. Not the timing. What is left is narrower and a good deal tougher, and it is the part that neither side says out loud very often, because it suits neither.

The smaller true thing

The Sun's demonstrated ceiling sits far above anything in the instrumental record. The magnetic shield is measurably weakening, faster in places than the global average. And the consequences of those two together are underpriced in public planning. None of that needs a micronova, and all of it comes from the mainstream.

That is a smaller claim than the frontier makes, and a much larger one than the dismissals allow. On the evidence, it is also the true one.
